# Aircraft Doppler in recordings and how to suppress it

Research snapshot · 8 November 2026

For DopplerSpoof, the primary engineering question is whether a release still lets an analyst recover the real location or the original flyover timing. A decoy is an optional audio effect whose privacy value needs to be measured against suppression alone. The smallest useful prototype is a local post-production tool that marks likely aircraft tracks, suppresses them across the channels being exported, and audits the decoded export. Full audio replacement provides a control and a fallback when the original sound is expendable.

## What Colsto demonstrated

Colsto describes identifying two faint flyover curves in a security-camera recording, then using the known 920-second interval between them to search historical traffic. The reported method used aircraft type to select plausible propeller traffic, a 200 m listener grid, and terrain elevation to compute 3D closest approach and the acoustic delay. Initial frequency measurements constrained speed and slant range. The search also considered places where extra aircraft should have been audible during an observed quiet interval. Final scoring compared the predicted retarded-time curves to the recorded spectrogram, adjusting for measurement uncertainty in CPA time and emitted frequency while keeping the predicted event separation fixed. The creator reports a location within roughly 100 m in this example, and a first-ranked true location in a US-wide search of a roughly one-minute excerpt with known recording time. The shorter run illustrates the value of a tighter time prior. An independent reconstruction transcribes the on-screen comparison as one day for the two-event search and one hour for the single-event search, both over the US. The creator’s description calls the latter a known-time clip. The exact timestamp inputs and how the hour was used in scoring are unclear. In six synthetic worlds, the reconstruction reported zero first-ranked true flights with an hour-long search window. Those simulations measure sensitivity to the prior using different recordings and traffic. ## How the geometry becomes a signature

Let x be a fixed microphone position, p(tₑ) the source position at emission time, v(tₑ) the velocity, R(tₑ) = ‖p(tₑ) − x‖, and c the effective sound speed. For still air and constant c,

- **Arrival:** tᵣ = tₑ + R(tₑ)/c.
- **Range rate:** Ṙ(tₑ) = (p(tₑ) − x) · v(tₑ) / R(tₑ). Positive means receding.
- **Received frequency:** fᵣ(tᵣ) = fₑ(tₑ) / [1 + Ṙ(tₑ)/c].

The frequency follows by differentiating the emitted phase with respect to arrival time. Use the source position at **emission** time. At a 1 km range, c = 343 m/s gives about 2.92 s of delay. A subsonic source gives a monotonic arrival map, so a simulator can map an emission-time trajectory to arrival times and interpolate, or solve the scalar equation per arrival sample. Under this model the audible CPA occurs at geometric CPA plus R(CPA)/c. Temperature, wind and refracted or reflected paths add error to the straight-ray model. [Ferguson’s 1992 propeller-aircraft study](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.403934) · [Ferguson and Quinn’s aircraft localization study](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/application-of-the-short-time-fourier-transform-and-the-wigner-vi/) · [NTSB analysis correcting a recorded aircraft tone with ADS-B](https://data.ntsb.gov/Docket/Document/docBLOB?FileExtension=pdf&FileName=ERA20LA179+SoundSpectrum_4222021-Rel.pdf&ID=12193294)

For a straight constant-speed pass, let τ be seconds since geometric CPA, v the speed and d the CPA slant distance. Then R = √(d² + v²τ²) and Ṙ = v²τ/R. When both tails are far enough from CPA and emitted frequency f₀ stays steady, f_hi ≈ f₀/(1 − v/c) and f_lo ≈ f₀/(1 + v/c), giving v ≈ c(f_hi − f_lo)/(f_hi + f_lo). At the received CPA, fᵣ = f₀ and dfᵣ/dtᵣ = −f₀v²/(cd). For example, with f₀ = 100 Hz, v = 60 m/s and d = 500 m, the asymptotes are about 121.2 and 85.1 Hz and the CPA slope is about −2.1 Hz/s. A clip with only a short segment near CPA strongly couples v² and d. Turning, climbing and engine RPM changes confound a constant-speed, constant-frequency fit. Use a joint uncertainty region for initial search, then model the actual trajectory.

A propeller with B blades turning at RPM produces a blade-pass component at B × RPM / 60 Hz and can produce multiple harmonics. The visible ridge may belong to an overtone, a shaft-order component, or one engine of several. The emitted frequency and the Doppler factor multiply each other. A constant pitch adjustment scales the frequency and its slope together and preserves the dimensionless Doppler shape. A passing ground vehicle, an RC aircraft or changing motor speed may produce lookalike ridges. [Ferguson and Quinn](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/application-of-the-short-time-fourier-transform-and-the-wigner-vi/) · [AeroSonicDB collection and labeling study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06368)

## A reproducible attack to use as a test instrument

1. **Recover the media timeline and all audio channels.** Decode to PCM and retain presentation timestamps, sample rate, stream identity and evidence of missing intervals. Account for cuts, resampling, stream gaps, constant clock offset and drift. Treat container timestamps as leads. Examine channels independently and also their downmix. [FFmpeg stream selection and metadata](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-all.html) · [ffprobe](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffprobe-all.html)
2. **Extract candidate events.** Use overlapping Hann-window STFTs, a local noise-floor estimate, and harmonic ridge tracing. An exploratory combination is 0.5–2 s windows with shorter hops and a separate shorter-window view around rapid transitions. A long window separates near frequencies but smears faster sweeps. FFT zero-padding increases displayed sampling density while the analysis window still limits the ability to separate adjacent tones. Plot raw and whitened power, retain multiple ridge hypotheses and record confidence and missing intervals. Try manual tracing as a stronger baseline than the app’s own automatic detector. [SciPy ShortTimeFFT](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.ShortTimeFFT.html) · [Dopplertrack detector](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack)
3. **Ingest historical trajectories.** Save provenance, UTC, position and velocity source, type and altitude fields. adsb.lol publishes daily readsb traces under ODbL. Partition tracks by time and position, stitch adjacent dates where needed, split gaps or implausible jumps, and interpolate within reliable segments. Use geometric altitude if available and compatible. Treat pressure altitude as a different quantity with an uncertainty budget. ICAO type designators and a sourced aircraft database help prioritize plausible aircraft. Keep unknown types in a lower-priority pass. Archive completeness depends on receiver coverage, data availability and aircraft equipage. [FAA ADS-B strategy](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/air_traffic/technology/adsb/quicklinks/ADS-B_In_Strategy.pdf) · [adsb.lol archive](https://www.adsb.lol/docs/open-data/historical) · [readsb trace schema](https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb/blob/dev/README-json.md) · [ICAO Doc 8643](https://store.icao.int/en/aircraft-type-designators-doc-8643)
4. **Generate candidates cheaply.** Convert positions to metric Earth-centered coordinates or suitable local tangent frames. For each point use a DEM elevation plus a microphone-height assumption. Check the vertical datum. USGS 3DEP is usually NAVD88, while ADS-B geometric height can be ellipsoidal. An incorrect conversion biases the inferred slant range. Use the fitted speed, time, and slant distance to prune trajectories. A known UTC event window limits the temporal search. Two events without exact UTC allow a spatial-temporal join on flight pairs, since the aircraft near each emission must be within the sum of the plausible slant ranges of each other. For a candidate, compare the **arrival** difference, (tₑ,₂ − tₑ,₁) + (R₂ − R₁)/c, to the clip interval. Candidate pruning should retain a tolerance for each approximation. A single straight flyover and known time can leave mirror solutions or elongated regions. Crossing track geometry and differences in altitude or turns may break these ambiguities. [USGS 3DEP datums](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-projection-horizontal-datum-vertical-datum-and-resolution-a-usgs-digital-elevation-model) · [Dopplertrack’s pair join](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack)
5. **Score in the arrival-time spectrogram.** Given candidate x, project each interpolated trajectory into predicted fᵣ(tᵣ). Evaluate whitened power along the fundamental and supported harmonics with robust aggregation, a small frequency corridor and penalties for missing support or excessive frequency flexibility. Optimize one shared clip-to-UTC offset per candidate. Bound each event’s emitted frequency and any allowed drift. Separate time offsets can reward an otherwise incompatible pair. Allow the time windows and the score to reflect missing frames. Use a soft penalty for predicted unobserved passes only where the archive, recording coverage and expected audibility support it. A binary silence gate can wrongly discard the true site when speech, masking, directivity or terrain hides a plane. This scorer is a proposed implementation.
6. **Retain uncertainty and a no-match outcome.** Refine high-scoring clusters locally and report alternative sites, curve overlays and score margins. Calibrate on negatives, wrong-day and wrong-clock controls, and holdout recordings. On a large search, the highest score exists even when every candidate is spurious. Compare known-time and date-only scenarios separately. Inspect how time, sound speed, aircraft availability, datum and ridge choices affect the candidate list.

## What ordinary audio edits leave behind

| Edit | What an adaptive analyst can still exploit |
| --- | --- |
| High-pass or fixed EQ | Harmonics above the cutoff and leakage through the transition band can carry the same timing and relative sweep. Raising the cutoff can remove voice body. |
| Add music, noise or another flyover | The original remains in the mixture. A narrow coherent ridge can accumulate evidence across time and harmonics, and a new foreground ridge can be ignored or peeled away. Perceived masking is a separate test from detector recovery. |
| Phone codecs and generic denoising | Codec mode, bitrate, band limits, AGC and suppression settings change what survives. A retained narrow tone still carries a signature. Test actual encode/decode paths instead of assuming that perceptual coding or speech enhancement erases it. |
| Global pitch or time shift | Fixed pitch scaling preserves relative contour shape. A common timeline shift preserves inter-event differences. Uniform time stretching changes the spacing, but an analyst can fit a scale or use surviving individual events, and AV sync can limit useful changes. |
| Cut a flyover or alter spacing | A complete removal can defeat that pair. Surviving tails, other passes and single-event fits with an external time prior may still locate the clip. Clean gap fill avoids carrying source audio into the cut. |
| Mute or rebuild the full audio track | Removing all original audio from the distributable file removes its aircraft ridge channel. A clean dialogue stem or re-recorded speech with unrelated room tone can preserve intelligibility where muting is unacceptable. |

The underlying DSP and codec behavior are documented by [SciPy](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.ShortTimeFFT.html), [FFmpeg’s audio filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html), [the Opus specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6716), and [Microsoft’s DNS Challenge](https://github.com/microsoft/DNS-Challenge). A published synthetic test applied a 300–3,400 Hz band limit and μ-law coding to a modeled propeller pass. Summed harmonics recovered the contour within 1 Hz in about 51% of frames with weak harmonics and 96.5% with strong ones. The simulation omitted phone-specific suppression and actual AMR encoding. [Synthetic filter and telephony experiments](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments/blob/main/docs/04-real-world-limits.md)

## Prototype design

**Inspect.** Import media locally, show the spectrogram and candidate harmonic families, and let the user draw or adjust regions where automatic detection is uncertain. Report which streams and channels have been inspected. This first version can operate without the recording’s real coordinates, UTC or a flight archive. Use the archive only for controlled robustness tests on authorized recordings.

**Suppress.** Start with soft moving masks around tracked harmonics in a complex STFT. Vary width by ridge uncertainty, taper mask edges across time and frequency, and reconstruct with a consistent inverse STFT. Keep aligned processing across exported channels. Test a tracked sinusoidal estimate and subtraction as an alternative when phase is reliable. Wider masks and multi-resolution analysis can help with broadened lines, reflections and drift. Where aircraft and speech share a bin, local masking trades residual aircraft energy for speech damage. Try a dialogue stem or replace affected spans with independent ambience if the post-edit audit still finds a ridge. Manual review should be possible when detection is uncertain. [SciPy reconstruction](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.ShortTimeFFT.html)

**Synthesize only after suppression.** For a neutral synthetic flyover, pick a generic source trajectory and compute tₑ(tᵣ). Evaluate an emitted signal at that retarded time, or generate sample-continuous harmonic phases by integrating the desired received instantaneous frequency. Render correlated harmonic envelopes, gently changing emitted RPM, distance-dependent amplitude, modest directionality and filtered broadband texture. Apply motion and delay once. A source signal can also be resampled at emission time, which Doppler-warps its broadband texture as well as the tones. Add the result as an editable separate layer. Avoid reusing extracted source tails or inserting another real recording whose timing or location is independently identifiable. A decoy engineered to match real flights at a chosen alternate place would require internally consistent multiple trajectories, arrival timing and a plausible soundscape. Keep this separate from a privacy guarantee. [NASA aircraft-noise synthesis report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180002090/downloads/20180002090.pdf) · [NASA flyover audio examples](https://stabserv.larc.nasa.gov/flyover/)

**Export and audit.** Create a fresh output with explicit audio and video stream selection. Use video stream copy when compatible, preserve intended timing, and inspect output streams, tags, chapters, attachments and secondary audio. Exclude original audio from alternate tracks, previews and release bundles. Decode the final deliverable and run a second inspection after platform-style recompression. Keep original and edited projects separate. Evaluate multiple public renders of the same source together. Aligning them and averaging can strengthen a persistent original ridge when randomized decoys differ across versions. Aircraft audio is one clue among GPS tags, timestamps, visuals, other environmental sounds and linkage across clips. [FFmpeg mapping](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-all.html) · [ffprobe](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffprobe-all.html)

## Validation before any privacy claim

| Test layer | Cases | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physics and export | Stationary tone, approaching/receding pass, changing altitude and heading, variable RPM, timestamp gaps, multiple audio streams | Arrival-time inversion error, ridge-frequency error, phase continuity, reconstruction error, AV sync and stream inventory |
| Known mixtures | Mix clean speech and ambience with isolated or synthetic passes. Vary level, window length, harmonics, overlaps, microphone response and platform encodes. Include clean negatives and false lookalikes. | Residual matched-filter score against original ridge, detection recall at a calibrated false-positive rate, local attenuation, dialogue quality |
| Controlled real recordings | Record consented fixtures with ground-truth microphone position, UTC and track data. Include multiple microphones and devices, independent sites and days, long and short clips, weak tracks, clutter and uncertain clocks. Hold out sites, aircraft and devices from tuning. | True-location rank, top-k within predeclared radii, error distance, candidate region, false confident matches and abstention rate |
| Privacy ablation | Compare original, high-pass, music, codec, suppression alone, decoy alone, suppression plus decoy and full replacement. Test known UTC, rough date, repeated clips and unknown clock. | Whether the real site remains recoverable, how far suppression changes its rank, and whether adding a decoy helps beyond suppression |
| Adaptive challenge | Analyze final encoded output with independent detector settings, multi-channel and harmonic pooling, manual annotation, peak removal and re-search, and a search that allows individual events or ignores decoys. | Residual recovery rates, score margins and failure examples. Separate model failure from missing archive data. |

Use a localized positive baseline before claiming a privacy improvement for a particular fixture. Report both all-clip results and the subset that could be located before editing. Set operating thresholds on held-out validation data and state the search assumptions alongside each result. Subjective speech quality, background intrusiveness and overall quality can follow ITU-T P.835. Word accuracy adds an intelligibility measure. Time-frequency attenuation and attack success remain the privacy measures. AeroSonicDB offers labeled aircraft and background audio for detector development. Pathak’s Livermore collection provides 40 phone WAVs, corresponding ADS-B tracks and annotated harmonic traces. The deposit does not give a surveyed microphone position. A localization benchmark also needs independently measured ground truth and enough surrounding candidate traffic to make the search meaningful. [ITU-T P.835](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-P.835) · [Microsoft DNS Challenge](https://github.com/microsoft/DNS-Challenge) · [AeroSonicDB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06368) · [Pathak’s Livermore dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/23424278)

Start by validating a synthetic retarded-time generator against the analytical pass. Exercise manual masks and export auditing on known mixtures and run a separate attacker on authorized real clips. Then evaluate automatic tracing and synthetic ambience against the suppression-only result. This order exposes how much of the privacy gain comes from actually removing recoverable evidence.
