# Generating realistic aircraft flyover audio

Research snapshot · 8 November 2026

For an aircraft sound that can fly in any direction, use real or measured aircraft sound as the **emitter**, then render how that sound reaches the virtual microphone. A complete flyover recording is a useful reference or an instant fixed-trajectory effect. It already contains the original aircraft’s Doppler shift, angle-dependent timbre, propagation and microphone perspective. An editable flyover needs an emission model with those effects applied for the new path. This report builds on the [Doppler audio privacy research](doppler-audio-privacy.md).

## Three ways to get the emitter sound

| Approach | What to build | What the result supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replay a whole recorded pass | Use a licensed flyover in its original timing. Adjust level and apply gentle stereo positioning only as a creative mix. | The actual recording sounds authentic. Direction and trajectory remain those captured in the sample. A mono pass can be panned, but that alone cannot change its approach geometry or nose-versus-tail timbre. |
| Sample-based emitter | Capture licensed stationary exterior recordings across power/RPM settings and front, side and aft angles. Select clean sections, create phase-aligned loops and interpolate their gains, spectra and, where useful, loop playback rates. | Most direct route to a recognizable named aircraft for a few presets. A static run-up misses forward-flight loading, propeller inflow, aerodynamic noise and some directivity. Use known flight recordings to tune those differences. |
| Procedural or measured hybrid | Generate phase-continuous rotor/engine tones and filtered noise. Fit harmonic levels, broadband spectral shape and directivity from measured audio. Optionally blend independently recorded, dry engine textures. | Continuous controls and variable duration. With sparse data it supports recognizable aircraft classes. A precise type and engine configuration require more measured references. |

Microsoft Flight Simulator documents a practical production workflow for recordings and crossfaded engine loops. NASA's work separates periodic or tonal synthesis and broadband noise, using measurements or noise models to drive them. DLR's PropNoise → VIOLIN → CORAL chain predicts source components, propagation and the audible signal separately. These are useful reference architectures with different accuracy and asset requirements. [Microsoft recording guide](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/msfs2024/html/4_Sound/Aircraft_Audio/Recording_Audio.htm) · [Microsoft engine audio setup](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/msfs2024/flighting/sound/aircraft-audio/engine-audio-setup/) · [NASA source models and synthesis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002351/downloads/20200002351.pdf) · [DLR simulation framework](https://www.dlr.de/en/at/research-transfer/research-infrastructure/aeroacoustics-research-infrastructure/aeroacoustic-simulation-framework)

### Extracting source behavior from a real pass

If a recording has a synchronized trajectory and known microphone position, compute tᵣ(tₑ) = tₑ + R(tₑ)/c and interpolate the recorded waveform at those arrival times to undo the direct path’s time warp. Estimate RPM/order contours, angle-dependent levels and broadband envelopes in source time. A de-Dopplerized pass still contains its changing propagation gain, ground interference, air absorption, background and the recorder’s response. Smoothly compensate for a modeled direct-path gain only where the recording supports it. Avoid inverse filtering deep comb nulls or boosting quiet tails. Prefer estimating curves and resynthesizing with fresh noise over using the whole warped waveform as a clean emitter. With one pass, emission angle changes at the same time as distance and possibly power, so its directional response cannot be independently recovered. Use multiple passes and different geometries, or calibrated measurements at fixed power and several angles. [NASA comparison of aircraft auralizations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200011450/downloads/20200011450.pdf) · [Empa sonAIR source model](https://www.empa.ch/fr/web/s509/sonair)

NASA demonstrated this workflow on an AS350 BA at 80 KIAS and 91.4 m above ground. It estimated angle-dependent periodic content from de-Dopplerized segments and synthesized the signal with fractional-delay propagation. The authors retained the measured atmospheric absorption in their reconstruction and skipped a second application of that effect. They also describe phase-alignment limits, including unfinished full-flyover phase modulation. The listening page gives the measured flyover (Audio 1), a de-Dopplerized segment (2), the synthesized source (6) and two propagated renders (7–8). [NASA AS350 study, §§ V–VI](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180002090/downloads/20180002090.pdf) · [NASA AS350 listening examples](https://stabserv.larc.nasa.gov/flyover/)

| Helicopter | Main and tail rotor orders, engine/gearbox layers and broadband. Use angle- and maneuver-dependent pulses or blade slap when supported by the reference; steady blade rate alone sounds static. | NASA’s examples include rotor noise across transitional flight, original and reconstructed signals, and variants with amplitude modulation. [NASA flyover and rotor examples](https://stabserv.larc.nasa.gov/flyover/) |

Blade-pass frequency is blade count × shaft RPM / 60. It anchors tonal spacing, and multiple aircraft can produce overlapping frequencies. Tone balance, spectral envelope, power and configuration transitions, and directional behavior carry much of the perceived identity. Store presets as source data with provenance and an accuracy label. A generic twin turboprop with Q400-like tonal spacing can be presented as a class preset until a measured Q400 reference supports a stronger claim.

## Controls and trajectory

- **Aircraft and phase** select emitter preset and operating curve. A first release can expose cruise, takeoff/climb and approach as editable profiles. Keep throttle or power separate from velocity. A constant-speed propeller can change thrust with little RPM change.
- **Direction of travel** is the track bearing in world space. **Pass side** and **cross-track offset** say where the track lies relative to the microphone. **Listener or camera heading** defines what sounds left, front or right. **Source orientation** determines which part of the aircraft is facing the microphone. For straight flight in still air, assume the aircraft points along its track. Keep the concepts separate so turns, climb and yaw can be added later.
- **Speed, height, closest approach time and gain** establish the event. Specify height above the microphone for the local straight-flight preset and signed horizontal offset in metres. Use an editor control for the **heard** CPA. Video geometry can separately show when the aircraft physically passes, since sound arrives later.
- **Environment and output** can offer dry outdoor first, then soft or hard ground, camera-perspective mono, stereo, and headphone HRTF. Add wind, obstacle or room effects only where the scene requires them. The initial renderer can use a uniform atmosphere.

One coordinate convention is East, North, Up with compass angle θ clockwise from north. Let the track unit vector be (sin θ, cos θ, 0), the right-of-track vector be (cos θ, −sin θ, 0), and the listener be at the origin. For height h, signed offset b and speed v, use source position p(tₑ) = b · right + (0,0,h) + v(tₑ − tCPA) · track. The CPA slant distance is √(b²+h²). For example, an eastbound pass at 300 m height and 400 m horizontal offset has 500 m slant range at CPA. At 343 m/s the listener hears the CPA about 1.46 s after it occurs. Facing north, an eastbound pass moves left to right. Reversing the track while holding the same physical path mirrors the sweep. If b is stored relative to travel direction, reverse its sign too when reversing the track to keep the path on the same side of the microphone.

## Rendering the moving sound

1. **Make source-domain pressure.** For a procedural propeller, integrate one shaft phase over time. Multiply by blade count for the blade-pass phase and by harmonic order for overtones. Change amplitude and spectrum continuously, with correlated RPM and load variation. Use band-shaped noise for airframe and turbulent components. For samples, maintain loop phase through crossfades and use fixed source-state loops or interpolate a continuous engine sound. Interpolate angular directivity per band or component in aircraft body coordinates. An overall volume cone can approximate a single broad direction, while measured angle-specific spectra convey nose, broadside and tail differences.
2. **Render arrival-time audio.** For a stationary microphone x and a subsonic source in still air, solve tᵣ = tₑ + ‖p(tₑ) − x‖/c. Precompute a monotone table on the trajectory and invert it, or bracket and solve sample/block intervals. A fractionally interpolated delay line or band-limited time-varying resampler reads the source at tₑ. Its rate dtₑ/dtᵣ = 1/(1 + Ṙ/c) creates the Doppler shift of both tones and noise. For the full waveform path, omit a separate Doppler pitch plugin. An analytic oscillator can instead integrate the received frequency per sample, with the emitted state still evaluated at tₑ. Use band-limited interpolation and headroom for the largest approach ratio to avoid aliasing. Preserve histories and interpolation continuity across DSP blocks and between preview and export. [NASA time-marching and propagation comparison](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200011450/downloads/20200011450.pdf)
3. **Apply propagation gain and filtering.** With a source amplitude referenced at R_ref, begin with pressure gain R_ref/R, bounded at close range. Apply distance-dependent high-frequency loss with smooth, band-dependent filters. A ground reflection uses a separate image-source path, its own retarded time, angle, spreading and frequency-dependent reflection coefficient. Sum the paths as waveforms so interference emerges. A basic phase-free EQ or short reverb can approximate character where exact ground properties are unknown. Avoid counting distance loss both in the renderer and spatializer. Avoid dynamic normalization that flattens the approach/recession envelope. [NASA propagation comparison](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200011450/downloads/20200011450.pdf)
4. **Spatialize from the retarded bearing.** Convert the direct path's incoming direction from p(tₑ) into listener coordinates using camera yaw, pitch and roll. Stereo equal-power panning works for left/right previews. Use HRTFs for headphone direction and elevation; render individual path directions if reflections matter. The same geometrical path can yield very different perceived motion if the camera turns. A fixed mono output carries range, timing and source timbre cues. Rotating the entire path around the microphone preserves the ideal mono signal in an azimuthally symmetric scene. The absolute compass bearing then requires visual or other context. [W3C Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/) · [RWTH Virtual Acoustics rendering](https://ita.pages.rwth-aachen.de/VAWebsite/VA/documentation/rendering.html)
5. **Make interactive updates continuous.** For a timeline editor, pre-roll the emission model by the maximum modeled delay in the preview interval and render buffers ahead. For a live flyover, retain past trajectory and emitter state in a ring buffer. Apply direction changes to future emissions with bounded turn rate and smoothed control values, while already emitted sound continues arriving from its historical direction. A user dragging the route may prefer an audition mode that regenerates a new scene and crossfades. For quicker audition, shift the preview by one common time offset so playback can begin at the first audible arrival. This preserves direct-versus-reflected timing. Physical live mode retains the real causal delay.

A practical first implementation is an offline deterministic mono/stereo WAV renderer, then a preview using the same math. A browser can run the source and time-varying delay in an AudioWorklet and use a PannerNode for equal-power or HRTF rendering. The Web Audio API removed built-in Doppler. If the worklet applies distance loss and directivity, set panner rolloff to zero and keep its source cone omnidirectional. For repeatable binaural export across platforms, pin the HRTF dataset and implementation instead of relying on browser-specific defaults. Web Audio's AudioBufferSourceNode playbackRate is k-rate, which is sufficient for a rough audition and gives less control than a dedicated fractional reader. [W3C Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/) · [Web Audio working group transition notes](https://github.com/WebAudio/web-audio-api/blob/main/webaudio-CR-transition.md) · [RWTH Virtual Acoustics](https://ita.pages.rwth-aachen.de/VAWebsite/VA/index.html)

For video, export an isolated WAV stem aligned to the clip timeline and mix it into the cleaned audio. Playing the flyover through a physical speaker and re-recording it adds the speaker response, playback room, microphone and gain control. A single fixed speaker also fixes the apparent direction at the recording position. Stereo speakers can suggest lateral motion for a listener in a suitable position. A headphone HRTF mix is better kept as a playback or export format for headphones.

## Validate that it sounds like a pass

- **Physics fixtures.** A stationary emitter retains its source pitch. A straight 80 m/s pass with a 100 Hz emitted tone, 1,000 m slant CPA and c = 343 m/s has distant approach/recede limits of about 130.4/81.1 Hz, an acoustic CPA delay of 2.92 s and a local slope near −1.87 Hz/s. Test emitted RPM changes separately, compare simulated and analytical ridges, and verify sample continuity across control changes and loop wraps.
- **Direction fixtures.** Render the same world trajectory with listener yaw rotated 180° and check the corresponding change in relative bearing and channel motion. Render the mirrored path separately. Compare near-horizontal, oblique and overhead paths. On headphones, evaluate front/back and elevation confusion in addition to left/right. Verify mono compatibility and ensure binaural output preserves the requested timing.
- **Reference comparisons.** At a matched aircraft class, phase and geometry, compare level-versus-time, harmonic trajectories and relative harmonic levels, band spectra before/during/after CPA, and transient or modulation behavior. Match the intended microphone perspective. A distant aircraft heard on a phone video should have a different apparent spectrum and level than a clean close-range headphone demo. Compare calibrated levels only when the reference provides calibration.
- **Listening tests.** Randomize measured clips, procedural renders and hybrid renders. Have listeners identify class, apparent path/direction, and obvious synthetic artifacts in both isolated playback and the intended video mix. Match overall presentation level while preserving each clip’s internal envelope. Include held-out passes so the comparison does not reward a memorized recording. In a DLR and Empa listening comparison, participants labeled the two simulations as recordings about 40% and 20% of the time. Both remained distinguishable overall, even with similar sound exposure levels. This is a reason to test timbre and motion perceptually as well as checking spectra and levels. [NASA multi-tool auralization comparison](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200011450/downloads/20200011450.pdf) · [DLR perception study](https://elib.dlr.de/200727/1/ELIB-Eintrag-2023-BertschlL-200727-PaperPublished.pdf)
- **Privacy and release.** Mix a generated flyover onto a track that has already passed a residual-ridge audit. Compare suppression alone against suppression plus the render, including the final encoded file. Keep generated audio independent of the original flyover’s samples, timing and geometry. The older report defines the adaptive attacker and release checks. [Doppler audio privacy research](doppler-audio-privacy.md)

Start with one light piston preset. Render a repeatable straight pass with selectable direction, side, height and speed. Compare it against measured passes using the same geometry and a simple sample-based baseline. Once the renderer passes timing, direction and listening checks, fit measured state and angle profiles for additional presets. This isolates the acoustic engine from the ongoing work of collecting convincing aircraft assets.
